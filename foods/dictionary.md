# Food dictionary

Personal food database. Every food that appears in the log has a row here.
All nutrient values are **per 100 g** (ml for liquids); the portion column is
this user's usual serving. Source: `label` (see `foods/labels/`) or
`estimate` (generic USDA-typical values). **Key micros** lists the tags from
`foods/micronutrients.md` that a usual portion meaningfully provides.

`Sugar` is total sugar; `Added` is the free-sugar part only (added in
production or preparation, plus honey, syrups and fruit juice). Sugar
naturally present in whole fruit, vegetables and milk is not counted as
added.

Log rows must use the exact name from this table.

## Protein density — the shortlist

During the cutting phase the binding constraint is **protein per 100 kcal**,
not protein per 100 g. Reach for the top of this list when the day is short
on protein and short on calories. Ready-to-eat items are marked ▪ — they
work at the office, where no cooking is possible.

**Canned oily fish is the best single choice when omega-3 or vitamin D are
also overdue** — sardines close three gaps at once (protein, ω3, calcium
from the bones) even though their ratio is below tuna's. Do not narrow the
search to dairy just because the conversation started there.

yogurt, diluted rather than concentrated. Strained or protein-fortified is
what matters, not the fat percentage.
