## The pattern that matters

Protein density, meal by meal, is the single clearest finding:

| Meal | kcal | Protein | Protein / 100 kcal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sun breakfast (vinete, lipie, cașcaval, tobă…) | 982 | 43.9 | 4.5 g |
| Sat brunch (ochiuri, cartofi, pâine, dulceață…) | 1758 | 57.3 | 3.3 g |
| **Sun dinner (300 g chicken + spinach)** | **693** | **98.9** | **14.3 g** |

Meals are built around bread, vegetables and good fats, with protein as an
afterthought. That is why fat averages 112 g against a 65 g target while
protein lags. The fix is compositional, not about measuring more precisely:
**anchor each meal on the protein, then build around it.** Sunday's dinner
proves it works — it delivered more protein than the whole of Saturday's
brunch, in 40 % of the calories.

**Open hypothesis:** late, fat-heavy or spicy dinners fragment the second
half of the night. Two data points, and the good night also had a full gym
session, a swim and sauna — every one of which independently improves sleep.
Not yet attributable to the meal. A clean test would be an early, light
dinner on a day *without* training.

Caffeine and nicotine are permanently absent (he drinks no coffee and does
not smoke), which removes the two usual confounders from this analysis.
